## 1. Case study — DuSu

### 1.1 The product in one page (✔ everything here is read from the repo as of 2026-10-17)

**DuSu** is a **voice-first AI English-speaking coach** — tagline *"Speak with confidence."* The learner talks out loud; DuSu listens, answers in a warm woman's voice, remembers them, and keeps the conversation going. It is a *companion*, not a course: no flashcards, no tapping answers. It trains the thing people actually freeze on — **speaking to another human under pressure**.

- **Where it lives:** a web app / installable PWA at `https://dusu.ranabrothers.online` (legacy `dusu.ruralrootcloud.com` still works). **No app-store listing yet.** Sign-in is Google. Voice needs **Chrome or Edge** (Web Speech API) — Android Chrome and laptop Chrome/Edge are the safe claim.
- **Who makes it:** *Rana Brothers* — two brothers from India building an independent project. **Not a company** (brand rule, existing plan §1).
- **Cost to the learner:** **free today**, with one honest catch — each learner connects **two of their own free AI keys** (Gemini / Groq / OpenRouter / GitHub), one time, ~2 minutes, with in-app video guides. That is *why* it is free to use (each conversation still has turn limits, and each AI provider has its own free quota). A paid tier (₹299/₹599/₹899) is drafted on a placeholder screen but **no payment exists**.
- **Privacy, in the product's own words (✔ `privacy.html`, updated 2026-10-16):** in the talk modes (Daily Talk, Face-to-Face, Interview, Learn) and the Practice Room's viva / group-discussion / role-play, DuSu "does not record, receive or store your voice" (the browser does the speech-to-text; the browser vendor's online speech service may process audio under its own policy). **The recorded modes are different:** Practice Room presentation / speech / seminar and Job Interview Practice record your answers — the recording stays on your phone, not on DuSu's servers, and is sent to a speech-to-text provider (Groq, or Google Gemini) to turn it into text; Job Interview Practice also stores the resume / job-description text (contact details removed, best effort) and your answers' transcripts until you delete them. No ads; no third-party analytics; DuSu does not use your conversations to train any model (the AI provider you connect may keep what it receives on a free tier, under its own policy); delete-my-account exists; Practice Room / Job Interview Practice / Grammar Coach are for **18+**.

### 1.2 The audience and the insight

**Primary wedge (existing plan §4):** Indian **final-year students and freshers in placement season** — they *know* English, they freeze when they must *speak* it to a human; budget Android phone, patchy data, a drive in 4–10 weeks. **Also:** freshers 0–2 yrs (LinkedIn/Instagram), non-metro Hindi-first learners (YouTube/Instagram), working professionals (meetings/stand-ups), and — later — college training & placement cells. **Not our user:** IELTS/TOEFL score-chasers, absolute beginners, anyone under 13.

**The insight that drives every hook: the freeze is fear, not ignorance.** Smart people go blank because of **fear of being judged** ("log kya kahenge"), because they **translate in their head**, and because they have **no private place to be bad at it**. DuSu is that place. (🔎 Early academic work suggests AI speaking partners can lower speaking anxiety and raise willingness to talk, but results are small-sample and mixed — so we say "may help", never "cures".)

| Emotion | What they think | What content should do |
|---|---|---|
| Embarrassment | "Sab hansenge" | Laugh **with** them; show that the audience is smaller than they feel (spotlight effect) |
| Time pressure | "Placement 6 hafte mein hai" | Give a 5-minute daily step; never scare |
| Class shame | "Hindi-medium hoon, mujhse nahi hoga" | Pride framing: *you already think in two languages* |
| Perfectionism | "Perfect sentence ban jaye tab bolunga" | "Pehle bolo, phir sudharo" |
| Distrust | "Free? AI? Catch kya hai?" | Total transparency (privacy page, the keys explanation) |
| Aspiration | "Mujhe achhi job chahiye" | Visible progress, tiny wins |
| Belonging | "Kya sab aise hi hain?" | Rituals and a shared vocabulary (Speak Saturday, "freeze", "galti-point") |

**Job to be done:** *"When I have an HR round coming and I freeze, give me a safe place to rehearse out loud and show me exactly what to fix — so I walk in calm."*

### 1.6 So what — the strategy in six sentences

## 2. Human psychology — Hook · Story · Curiosity · Engagement · Connection

### 2.1 The five levers (every post uses all five; the Lever column in §12 names the main principle)

| Lever | Job | Tactic | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | Stop the scroll in 2 seconds | Pain mirror, pattern interrupt, contrarian line, a specific number, a 10-second challenge, "the end first" | The *Hook* column of every row |
| **Story** | Keep them past second 5 | Recurring characters (DuSu, Rohan), the Hook→Tension→Turn→Payoff shape, serial arcs | Sunday *Rohan ki Diary*; every skit |

| **Engagement** | Turn watching into doing | One micro-action per post ("say this sentence now"), "send this to the friend who…", polls, save-worthy copy-paste answers | CTA codes 0–4 (§5.1) |
| **Connection** | Make them belong | Shared words ("freeze", "galti-point"), rituals (Speak Saturday), the brothers' honesty, DuSu's warmth, replies in a human voice | Founder posts, comment replies, challenges |

### 2.2 Psychology principles we design with

*Established behavioural-science concepts — replication varies, so they are **design heuristics we test (§5.4), not guarantees**.*

| Principle (source) | In plain words | How DuSu content uses it | Sample hook (Hinglish) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spotlight effect** (Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky, 2000; for nerves the closer study is the illusion of transparency, Savitsky & Gilovich, 2003) | We think others notice our slips far more than they do | Normalise mistakes; the audience is smaller than you feel | "Log tumhari galti utni notice nahi karte jitna tum sochte ho." |
| **Information-gap curiosity** (Loewenstein, 1994) | A visible gap in knowledge makes us want to close it | Hooks that state the gap, then pay it off | "Ek chhota sa word badlo — tumhara jawab professional lagega." |
| **Open loops / Zeigarnik** (1927; lab replications mixed — cliffhangers are standard serial practice) | Unfinished things nag at us | Sunday cliffhangers; daily "tomorrow" teaser | "Rohan ka result kal aayega…" |
| **Benign-violation humour** (McGraw & Warren, 2010) | Funny = something wrong **and** safe at once | Skits about the freeze, literal-translation fails; laugh *with* the learner | "Interview se pehle mera brain 🧠" |
| **Pratfall effect** (Aronson, Willerman & Floyd, 1966) | A capable source that blunders slightly becomes *more* likeable | Day 102 bloopers; "our AI video got this wrong" | "Hamare AI ne ye galat bola — dekho." |
| **Endowed progress** (Nunes & Drèze, 2006) **and the goal-gradient effect** (Hull, 1932; Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng, 2006) | People push harder when they see they've already started | "Day 3 of 30", streak, level bars | "30 mein se 3 din ho gaye." |

### 2.4 Story shapes and joke shapes

**Story shapes:** (1) **Hook → Tension → Turn → Payoff → CTA** (default); (2) **Problem → Agitate → Solve**; (3) **Before → After → Bridge**; (4) **"Day N of …" serial** (challenge, Rohan); (5) **Skit in 3 beats:** setup → fumble → fix.

**Joke shapes the AI should use** (AI humour drifts bland — specificity is the cure): relatable observation with a *specific* detail ("Mummy asks 'khana khaya?' — and Rohan translates it word for word for the HR"); expectation vs reality; rule-of-three escalation; **literal translation fails** (Hindi idiom → word-for-word English); a character's voice (Mummy, HR Madam, roommate Aman, Rohan, DuSu's gentle "arre wah!"); the freeze itself ("my brain: 5 tabs, 0 sentences").

**The laugh-with test (every Thursday script must pass all three):** (1) Would a nervous learner feel *seen*, not shamed? (2) Is the joke on the *situation*, never on an accent, region, caste, gender, religion or body? (3) Does DuSu stay warm — she never mocks a user (✔ product rule: no judging)? No romance, ever (✔ DuSu is a coach/companion; keep everything PG).

### 2.5 Shareability check (Berger's STEPPS) and the "send test"

Social currency (does sharing make me look funny/smart?) · Triggers (cued by a daily situation: an interview, mummy's call) · Emotion (amusement, awe, anxiety → relief) · Public (visible habit) · Practical value (save-worthy) · Stories. **Send test:** *would someone send this to one named friend?* — Instagram's guidance reportedly weights **sends per reach** highly 🔎, so every Reel carries a "send this to the friend who…" line.

### 2.6 The seconds map

| Asset | 0–2 s | 2–10 s | middle | last 5 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Reel / Short (25–40 s)** | Hook (on-screen text 5–7 words + voice) | Tension / the wrong version | Payoff: the fix, the laugh, or the 8-second **real DuSu clip** | Reaction line → CTA → "kal: <next topic>" |
| **YouTube video (2–4 min)** | Hook + promise ("is video ke end tak…") | Story / why this hurts | 3 examples → real DuSu demo → 30-s recap | CTA → teaser of tomorrow |
| **LinkedIn post (900–1,300 chars)** | First line ≤ 140 chars (what shows before "…see more") | Short paragraphs, one concrete example | One takeaway | A question that invites a real comment (15+ words) |

### 2.7 Ethics rule

We persuade by **relief and progress**, never by shame, fear, fake scarcity or fake urgency. No "last chance", no "HR will reject you", no invented statistics, no comparing people. If a line makes a nervous learner feel smaller, it is cut.

> "I am agree."
>
> You've probably heard it in a meeting. Maybe you've said it. It isn't a knowledge problem — it's a translation habit.
>
> Hindi says "main sehmat hoon" — a state, so "am" feels natural. In English the idea already lives inside the verb: I agree.
>
> Two different rules hide behind the "am" habit:
>
> 1) "Agree" is already a verb, so it needs no "am".
>    I am agree → I agree
>
> 2) Verbs of possession and knowing are normally not used in the continuous.

>    I am knowing the answer → I know the answer
>    (Activities are fine: "I'm having lunch.")
>
> A small habit that helps: when you hear yourself put "am" before a verb, pause and say the sentence once more. That is the whole exercise.
>
> None of this makes anyone a weaker professional. It only makes the start of a sentence sound less certain than you are — and the fix is a day of noticing.
>
> Which of these did you hear first at work? And what would you add to the list?
>
